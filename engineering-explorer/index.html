Engineering Insurance

Role & responsibilities

    Key relationships

    Map discipline: radial links describe ecosystem roles and interfaces, not proof that every party participates in every project, policy, claim or placement.

    Contractors’ All Risks (CAR)

    Project cover for civil construction works against sudden and accidental physical loss or damage, subject to the policy wording, exclusions, project period and declared contract value.

    • Buildings, roads, bridges and civil works
    • Materials, temporary works and site facilities where insured
    • Third-party liability may be included or separately structured
    • Maintenance period and testing extensions require review

    Erection All Risks (EAR)

    Cover for installation and erection of mechanical or electrical plant, including defined storage, assembly, testing and commissioning phases.

    • Power, industrial and process equipment
    • Contractor, supplier and principal interests
    • Testing and commissioning conditions
    • Interface with marine cargo and transit cover

    Contractors’ Plant & Machinery (CPM)

    Physical damage cover for declared contractors’ mobile plant and machinery, whether owned or hired, subject to item schedules, use, location and wording.

    • Excavators, cranes, loaders and construction equipment
    • Owned, leased or hired-in equipment distinctions
    • Inland transit and off-site use require verification

    Machinery Breakdown (MBD)

    Sudden and unforeseen physical damage to insured machinery from defined mechanical, electrical or operational causes, subject to exclusions and maintenance conditions.

    • Rotating machinery, motors, pumps and production lines
    • Repair, replacement and expediting costs if covered
    • Gradual deterioration and wear exclusions need review

    Machinery Loss of Profits (MLOP / MBLP)

    Consequential financial loss following insured machinery damage, commonly dependent on a valid material-damage trigger and an agreed indemnity period.

    • Gross profit or other defined financial measure
    • Indemnity period, trends and increased cost of working
    • Waiting period and dependencies on MBD wording

    Electronic Equipment Insurance (EEI)

    Physical damage cover for specified electronic and electrical equipment; data restoration and increased cost of working may require extensions.

    • IT, control, diagnostic and communications systems
    • Portable equipment and off-site use
    • Data, cyber and business interruption are not automatically included

    Boiler & Pressure Vessel (BPV)

    Cover for defined explosion or collapse exposures involving boilers and pressure vessels, with inspection, statutory compliance and maintenance requirements.

    • Pressure plant, boilers and associated equipment
    • Inspection records and safety compliance
    • Third-party damage and business interruption require wording review

    Delay in Start-Up / ALOP (DSU)

    Financial loss caused by delay to project completion or commercial operation following insured physical damage, subject to a defined insured delay and indemnity basis.

    • Project schedule and critical-path analysis
    • Start-up date, waiting period and indemnity period
    • Dependencies on CAR/EAR material-damage cover

    Deterioration of Stock (DOS)

    Loss or damage to temperature-sensitive stock following insured refrigeration or machinery breakdown, subject to temperature, storage and notification conditions.

    • Cold storage and process refrigeration
    • Temperature logs and stock valuation
    • Waste, contamination and power interruption wording

    Civil Engineering Completed Risks

    Specialist cover for defined completed infrastructure assets after construction, where offered and subject to asset, maintenance and operational conditions.

    • Bridges, tunnels, roads and infrastructure
    • Operational hazards and inspection regime
    • Boundary with property all-risks and maintenance cover

    Project placement & engineering claims lifecycle

    Illustrative workflow; actual requirements depend on contract, policy wording, engineering facts and applicable law.

    01

    Project definition

    Owner, contractor, EPC scope, contract value, BOQ, site, design responsibility, programme and subcontractor structure.

    02

    Risk engineering

    Geotechnical data, design review, construction methods, site hazards, fire protection, quality controls and loss history.

    03

    Submission & underwriting

    CAR/EAR or operational cover selection, values, period, deductibles, extensions, warranties, exclusions and reinsurance support.

    04

    Policy inception & monitoring

    Bind confirmation, endorsements, updated schedules, progress milestones, extensions of time and material changes.

    05

    Incident & mitigation

    Safety response, protect people and property, notify insurer/broker, preserve damaged parts, logs, photos and site records.

    06

    Technical adjustment

    Cause investigation, policy trigger, scope of damage, repair methodology, reinstatement cost, salvage and expert reports.

    07

    Quantum & settlement

    Values, depreciation where applicable, underinsurance, deductibles, debris removal, expediting costs and interim payments.

    08

    Recovery & learning

    Subrogation, supplier/contractor recovery, reinsurance recoveries, root-cause analysis and risk-control improvements.

    09

    DSU / ALOP assessment

    Critical-path impact, insured damage trigger, delay attribution, mitigation, revenue assumptions and indemnity period.

    A construction defect, poor workmanship allegation or project delay is not automatically insured. Separate physical damage, legal liability, contractual responsibility and consequential financial loss; each requires its own policy and evidence analysis.

    • Gallagher UAE — published Middle East capability includes CAR/EAR, CPM, completed civil engineering risks, DSU/ALOP, environmental liability, operational all-risk, project cargo and decennial/inherent-defects cover. Capability document
    • Specialist engineering brokers — may coordinate project submissions, facultative placements, multinational programmes and claims advocacy; specific placements require transaction-level evidence.

    A broker capability statement is not proof of an appointment, insurer participation or a completed placement for a named project.

    Regulatory & technical context

    • CBUAE Rulebook, Article 2 — expressly lists engineering insurance and related liabilities within property and liability insurance business. Official classification
    • Dubai Municipality CAR model wording — a public sample document includes project-specific clauses such as cross liability, existing/surrounding property, maintenance, vibration/removal of support and underground services. Model wording PDF
    • Contract and authority requirements — project owners, lenders, free zones and competent authorities may specify insurance requirements; do not generalise a single model wording into a universal legal mandate.

    Regulatory classification does not itself establish that a particular engineering policy is compulsory for every project.

    Adjacent insurance lines & boundaries

    • Marine cargo — transit of imported equipment/materials before site delivery; distinguish transit cover from on-site CAR/EAR.
    • Property / operational all risks — completed assets after handover and during operation; check transition and overlap.
    • Public / employers’ liability — third-party and employee legal liability; not identical to engineering material-damage cover.
    • Professional indemnity — design/consultancy errors and omissions; defective design exclusions in CAR/EAR require close review.
    • Cyber / electronic data — EEI physical damage does not automatically provide cyber incident or data-loss protection.
