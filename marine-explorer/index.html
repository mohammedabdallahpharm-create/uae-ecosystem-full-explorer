Marine Insurance

Role & responsibilities

    Key relationships

    Map discipline: roles and connections are analytical architecture, not a claim that every participant is involved in every placement or claim. Confirm counterparties, contracts, licences and policy wording case by case.

    Marine Cargo

    Physical loss of or damage to goods while in transit. UAE insurer materials describe sea, air and land transit and, in some products, warehouse-to-warehouse structures.

    • General / containerised cargo
    • Bulk cargo and commodities
    • Project cargo / heavy lift
    • Stock throughput and temperature-sensitive cargo (subject to wording)

    Hull & Machinery

    Physical damage risks relating to the vessel, machinery and equipment, subject to insured value, navigation limits, conditions and exclusions.

    • Commercial and coastal vessels
    • Workboats, yachts and specialist craft
    • Shipbuilding / repair risks where offered
    • Loss of hire may be an optional extension

    Marine Liabilities

    Third-party liabilities arising from vessel ownership, operation, port and terminal activity, cargo handling or related maritime services.

    • Shipowner / charterer liability
    • Port and terminal operators
    • Stevedores and ship repairers
    • Freight forwarder liability

    Protection & Indemnity (P&I)

    A specialist mutual or commercial liability arrangement for shipowners and operators. The exact scope depends on club or insurer rules, entry terms and applicable limits.

    • Crew and third-party liabilities
    • Pollution and wreck-related exposures
    • Cargo liabilities and collision liabilities (subject to rules)
    • Distinguish P&I from Hull & Machinery

    War, Strikes & Special Perils

    Separate clauses or extensions may address war and strikes risks. Availability, geographic scope, cancellation and premium conditions require specific confirmation.

    Risk dimensions to underwrite

    Cargo description and packing, route and conveyance, vessel details, storage, transshipment, values, loss history, security and contractual allocation of responsibility.

    Core cargo claim evidence

    • Policy / certificate and shipment declaration
    • Commercial invoice and packing list
    • Bill of lading / airway bill / road consignment note
    • Delivery receipt, exception / shortage report and notices
    • Survey report, photographs and repair / disposal estimates
    • Temperature, handling and tracking records where relevant

    Hull claim evidence

    • Policy schedule, vessel particulars and class status
    • Deck / engine logs and voyage information
    • Master's report, incident and authority reports
    • Survey, repair quotations, invoices and sea trial records
    • Salvage, towage and general average documentation where applicable

    Market structure to map

    The marine insurance value chain crosses insurance capacity, specialist distribution, maritime trade, vessel operations, logistics infrastructure and technical services. Map named UAE entities only when their role and evidence are verified.

    • How are cargo descriptions, values and packing risks captured?
    • Where do survey, carrier and terminal records intersect?
    • How are recovery rights and notice deadlines tracked?

    Documented marine reinsurance relationship

    A court-recorded example, kept separate from current market capacity claims.

    ABNIC ↔ Arab War Risks Insurance Syndicate (AWRIS)

    Historical relationship — court documented

    • Ceding insurer: Al Buhaira National Insurance Company (ABNIC), Sharjah, UAE.
    • Reinsurance counterparty: Arab War Risks Insurance Syndicate (AWRIS), a Bahrain-incorporated syndicate.
    • Class / risk: Marine Hull War Risks, including war-related perils excluded from standard Hull & Machinery cover.
    • Structure: DIFC Court records describe a 100% facultative reinsurance placement in 2015, renewed annually through the policy period ending 9 June 2019.
    • Evidence boundary: This is proof of a specific historical relationship and placement, not evidence that the arrangement remains active in 2026 or that AWRIS currently offers capacity to other UAE insurers.

    Primary legal evidence: DIFC Court of Appeal — Al Buhaira National Insurance Company v AWRIS [2026] DIFC CA 003. This court record identifies ABNIC as writing Marine Hull and describes AWRIS as providing treaty and facultative marine war-risk reinsurance.

    Reinsurance mapping rule

    Only add a reinsurer relationship when a primary document, court record, annual report or official disclosure identifies the cedant, reinsurer, class and (where available) period / structure. A reinsurer's general marine page or market presence is not evidence of a specific treaty or facultative relationship.

    No current 2026 marine treaty panel or facultative placement has been asserted in this release. The ABNIC–AWRIS case is deliberately labelled historical.

    • Reinsurers: no UAE-market marine treaty / facultative relationship is asserted in this release without a primary source identifying the relevant reinsurer, cedant and class.
    • Shipping lines / carriers: intentionally not yet populated as named insurance counterparties. A carrier's presence in UAE trade is not evidence of its insurance role in a specific placement or claim.
    • P&I clubs: Skuld's published correspondent directory is used to evidence local correspondent relationships; club membership / cover is governed by the relevant club entry and rules, not inferred from a local correspondent listing.
