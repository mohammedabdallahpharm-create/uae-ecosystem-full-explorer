3. Three claim routes — do not treat them as identical

The exact steps depend on policy wording, liability, insurer procedures and the facts of the incident.

Route A

Third-party liability

Establish the incident and parties, collect required records, review liability and applicable cover, then route the claim for assessment or settlement under the relevant process.

Route B

Own damage / repair

Check policy cover, excess and exclusions; collect the accident report, damage evidence and estimate; then follow the insurer's assessment and approved repair arrangements where applicable.

Route C

Potential total loss

Requires a specific assessment of vehicle value, damage and policy terms; do not assume the repair pathway or settlement basis before the authorised assessment.

These are orientation-level pathways, not legal advice or a substitute for an insurer's current claim instructions.

Evidence to collect

Evidence first; interpretation second.

  • Policy schedule, limits, exclusions and excess.
  • Accident report, photos, repair estimates and assessment.
  • Claim frequency, severity, turnaround and rework data.

5. Value & opportunity — questions to validate

Potential friction points are hypotheses until supported by records.

  • Missing or inconsistent incident documentation.
  • Disagreement on liability, repair scope or valuation.
  • Parts and repair-network dependencies.

6. Official sources

  • CBUAE Rulebook · Insurance business classification — use to check the formal class taxonomy.
  • CBUAE · Licensing — verify licensed status and permitted activity.
  • DFSA · Insurance supervision — for relevant DIFC-regulated activity.
  • ADGM FSRA — check the relevant rulebook and regulated-entity register.
A regulatory classification is not evidence of market size, product availability, premium volume, loss performance or commercial opportunity. Keep documented facts separate from analysis and assumptions.
